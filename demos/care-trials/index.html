Problem portrait

Unknown callers upset Mum.

When the phone rings from a number she doesn’t know, she freezes, answers in a panic, or spends the afternoon unsettled — even if the call was harmless.

What families describe

  • “She picks up every unknown number, then can’t settle for hours.”
  • “Scam calls leave her convinced we’ve forgotten her.”
  • “I mute the phone and feel guilty — she still wants to feel reachable.”

Who it hits

Person living with dementia, primary caregiver, and anyone calling from work or new numbers.

Why it recurs

Recognition and trust shrink; the ringtone still means “answer.” Silence feels like abandonment.

Trial plan · 7 days

Trusted-caller trial

Allow only a short list of familiar numbers to ring through. Everything else goes to voicemail or a calm message.

  1. 1

    Setup

    Pick 4–6 trusted numbers (family, GP, neighbour). Add them to Contacts and turn on silence for unknown callers — or use a simple allow-list app.

  2. 2

    Effort

    ~20 minutes once, then a 2-minute evening note: Did she settle after any ring? Did you miss anything urgent?

  3. 3

    Safety

    Keep one known emergency path (neighbour / pendant / landline). Tell the GP surgery your preferred callback number. Review the voicemail once a day together if she wants.

  4. 4

    Success looks like

    Fewer panicked afternoon calls. She still hears from people she knows. You sleep easier without cutting her off from the world.

Day 4 · Quick check-in

How did the trial go today?

One tap is enough. Optional notes help the next family.

Outcome

Collective learning

What families learned

From 18 trusted-caller trials. Patterns, not promises — each home is different.

  • 12 helped — fewer afternoon spirals when only known voices rang.
  • 4 no difference — usually when the allow-list was too short or voicemail was never checked.
  • 2 harder — missed a church volunteer and a mobile GP callback.

Two variants emerging

Variant A

Trusted Calls Only

Strict allow-list. Best when scams or stranger rings are the main trigger.

Variant B

Church & Family Calls

Allow-list plus one “community” bucket (church office, volunteers) so familiar-but-not-family numbers still get through.
